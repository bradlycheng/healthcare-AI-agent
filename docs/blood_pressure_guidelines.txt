# Medical Reference: Blood Pressure Guidelines
# Source: Adapted from American Heart Association Guidelines

## Blood Pressure Categories

Blood pressure is measured in millimeters of mercury (mmHg) and expressed as systolic over diastolic pressure.

Normal Blood Pressure:
- Systolic: Less than 120 mmHg
- Diastolic: Less than 80 mmHg
- No treatment required, maintain healthy lifestyle

Elevated Blood Pressure:
- Systolic: 120-129 mmHg
- Diastolic: Less than 80 mmHg
- Lifestyle changes recommended, monitor regularly

Hypertension Stage 1:
- Systolic: 130-139 mmHg
- Diastolic: 80-89 mmHg
- Lifestyle changes and possible medication
- Increased risk of heart disease and stroke

Hypertension Stage 2:
- Systolic: 140 mmHg or higher
- Diastolic: 90 mmHg or higher
- Medication likely required along with lifestyle changes
- Significantly increased cardiovascular risk

Hypertensive Crisis:
- Systolic: Higher than 180 mmHg
- Diastolic: Higher than 120 mmHg
- MEDICAL EMERGENCY - Seek immediate care
- Risk of organ damage, stroke, heart attack

## Critical Blood Pressure Values

Dangerously Low (Hypotension):
- Systolic below 90 mmHg or Diastolic below 60 mmHg
- May cause dizziness, fainting, organ damage
- Requires medical evaluation

Dangerously High (Hypertensive Emergency):
- Systolic above 180 mmHg with symptoms
- Symptoms include: severe headache, chest pain, vision changes, confusion
- Call emergency services immediately

## Clinical Recommendations

For patients with BP above 180/120 mmHg:
1. Wait 5 minutes and remeasure
2. If still elevated, this is a hypertensive crisis
3. Check for symptoms of organ damage
4. Immediate medical evaluation required
5. Do not attempt to lower BP rapidly at home

For patients with consistently elevated BP:
1. Reduce sodium intake to less than 2,300 mg daily
2. Increase physical activity to 150 minutes per week
3. Maintain healthy weight (BMI 18.5-24.9)
4. Limit alcohol consumption
5. Follow prescribed medication regimen
