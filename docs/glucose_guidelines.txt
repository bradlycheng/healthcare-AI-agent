# Medical Reference: Blood Glucose Guidelines
# Source: Adapted from American Diabetes Association Standards of Care

## Normal Blood Glucose Levels

Fasting blood glucose levels:
- Normal: 70-99 mg/dL
- Prediabetes: 100-125 mg/dL
- Diabetes: 126 mg/dL or higher

Random blood glucose levels:
- Normal: Less than 140 mg/dL
- Prediabetes: 140-199 mg/dL  
- Diabetes: 200 mg/dL or higher

## Critical Values Requiring Immediate Attention

Hypoglycemia (Low Blood Sugar):
- Mild: 54-70 mg/dL - Patient may experience shakiness, sweating, confusion
- Severe: Below 54 mg/dL - Medical emergency, risk of seizures and loss of consciousness
- Critical: Below 40 mg/dL - Immediate treatment required, potential brain damage

Hyperglycemia (High Blood Sugar):
- Elevated: 180-250 mg/dL - May require medication adjustment
- High: 250-300 mg/dL - Check for ketones, contact healthcare provider
- Severe: Above 300 mg/dL - Medical emergency, risk of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
- Critical: Above 400 mg/dL - Immediate emergency care required

## HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin) Reference Ranges

- Normal: Below 5.7%
- Prediabetes: 5.7-6.4%
- Diabetes: 6.5% or higher
- Target for most diabetics: Below 7.0%
- Poorly controlled: Above 8.0%
- Dangerous: Above 10.0% - High risk of complications

## Clinical Recommendations

For patients with glucose above 300 mg/dL:
1. Check for ketones in urine
2. Ensure adequate hydration
3. Do not exercise if ketones are present
4. Contact healthcare provider immediately
5. May require emergency insulin therapy

For patients with glucose below 70 mg/dL:
1. Consume 15-20 grams of fast-acting carbohydrates
2. Recheck blood glucose after 15 minutes
3. Repeat treatment if still below 70 mg/dL
4. If unconscious, administer glucagon injection
5. Seek emergency care if unresponsive to treatment
